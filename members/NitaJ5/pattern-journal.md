# Pattern Journal · @NitaJ5

## Week 1 · Act like an owner, not a role

During the first week, we formed our team and divided the responsibilities. I became responsible for Discovery, but we agreed that this didn't mean we should only work on our own parts.

I wrote my problem statements, including one about how KIU students struggle to balance their university schedules with work and personal life. I also added my first outreach target and signed our team contract.

I helped with the README as well, even though it wasn't directly part of my Discovery role. At that point, we still had several different problems to choose from, so I wanted us to start with real situations rather than immediately thinking about a product.

**My contributions:** [Problem Pool](../../00-foundation/problem-pool.md), [Team Contract](../../00-foundation/team-contract.md), [README](../../README.md)

## Week 2 · Evidence over opinion

This week, we decided to investigate the campus laundry problem. At first, everyone had slightly different opinions about what the main issue was. I personally thought students wasted time because they couldn't easily know whether washing machines were available.

I added our initial ICP and worked on the problem hypothesis. I also prepared the Interview Script v1 and Outreach Tracker files, so we had somewhere to organize our questions and interviews.

What I understood was that our assumptions were not enough. We needed to talk to students and find out what actually happens when they do their laundry.

**My contributions:** [ICP](../../00-foundation/icp.md), [Problem Hypothesis](../../01-discovery/problem-hypothesis.md), [Interview Script v1](../../01-discovery/interview-script-v1.md), [Outreach Tracker](../../01-discovery/outreach-tracker.md)

## Week 3 · Kill your favorite idea

This week, I worked on our interview questions and added an update about our progress to DECISIONS.md. I also conducted interviews with students and helped document their experiences.

Before talking to students, we were mostly focused on laundry being left inside washing machines after the cycle finished. But the interviews showed that there were other problems too, like broken machines, long waiting times, and dryers that didn't work properly.

One thing that surprised me was that students reacted very differently. One student was still satisfied with the facilities, while another had stopped using them completely and started taking her laundry to Tbilisi.

I realized that just because students mentioned unattended laundry didn't mean our original prediction was completely correct. We still needed better evidence about how long people usually leave their clothes in the machines.

**My contributions:** [Interview Script v1](../../01-discovery/interview-script-v1.md), [Interview Logs](../../01-discovery/interview-logs/real/), [DECISIONS.md](../../DECISIONS.md)

## Week 4 · The prototype is the conversation

This week, I spent a lot of time working with our interview logs. I prepared the Affinity Map, Patterns Analysis, and Competitive Landscape Seed.

Instead of waiting until all six interviews were finished, I started with the first three. I grouped similar quotes together and tried to understand which problems appeared more than once. When Interview 04 was added, I updated those files again.

The fourth interview was especially interesting because one student described losing some of her clothes after someone moved them out of a washing machine. I hadn't really thought about that consequence before. It showed me that the problem wasn't always just about waiting.

I also updated our Problem Hypothesis with the interview evidence. For now, I think it's better to keep investigating instead of deciding too early what we should build.

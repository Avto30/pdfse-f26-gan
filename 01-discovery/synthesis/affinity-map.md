# Affinity Map

  
**Interviews analyzed:** 01, 02, 03, 04, 05, 06

## Pattern 1

**Pattern:** Students adjust their laundry schedules to quieter hours or less crowded days to avoid crowded machines and reduce waiting.

**Interviews behind it (2 minimum):** 01, 02, 03, 05, 06

**Quotes (verbatim, each tagged with its log number):**

- "When I first moved here, I used to do my laundry in the evenings, but then I realized that there were too many people at that time. Now I bring my laundry down at 7–8 in the morning, when there's nobody in the laundry room." (01)
- "To avoid having problems, I manage my time and carefully plan when to go downstairs." (01)
- "I mostly go in the mornings because that is when the machines are free and therefore washing them is easier for me, but I do not specifically wake up early in the morning for that reason." (02)
- "When there's no other option, I go there at 5 a.m. and finish around 9 a.m. I don't sleep in between because I'm afraid someone will take my laundry out." (03)

- "There have been times when I’ve stayed up because of my laundry or woken up early in the morning specifically to do it. Generally, washing machines are more available in the morning, when everyone is at university and stuff." (05; English translation)
- "I usually do laundry every 2 week, mostly on weekends, as there are less people on campus." (06)

## Pattern 2

**Pattern:** Students often rely on physical checks to find available machines, leading to repeated trips and wasted time, although some also use dormitory group chats.

**Interviews behind it:** 01, 02, 03, 04, 05, 06

**Quotes:**

- "There have been situations where all the working washing machines were occupied. This happens more often in Building G, almost all the time, but less frequently in Building F." (01)
- "We have a chat for our dormitory building where I can find out whether a washing machine is available, or I can simply go downstairs and check." (01)
- "I have to go up and down several times during some intervals to check if some machines are free" (02)
- "I do not have any ways to know whether washing machines are free rather than going to the laundry room and check it myself and having something that shows whether the washing machines or dryers are free, would make laundry days much easier and save a lot of time." (02)
- "You can't even do your laundry properly. When you go downstairs, there's no space available, so there's no point. You have to sit there and wait for someone to come down and free up a machine. And then you have to figure out which washing machine it is — is it broken? Does it spin properly?" (03)
- "Most of the time, all the washing machines are running. What can I do then? Nothing. I've left without washing my clothes that day." (03)
- "The only way I know to find out whether a washing machine is available is to go downstairs and check. There's no other way." (03)
- "When I go to check, I take my laundry with me, because if I go back upstairs, someone will definitely get there before me. I've gone downstairs and found three machines available, but after going back upstairs and returning, all three were occupied." (03)
- "Some people exhibit inhuman tendencies, like when they use the 3 hour modes to wash their clothes, like when you go down and occupy 3 washing machines and run 3 hour cycles on all of them while a hundred people are waiting for you when you are going to come back..." (04)
- "What I actually feel and bothers me and I lose a lot of time doing it is when I take my clothes down and none of the machines are free, or someone has left their clean laundry in the machine unattended, or there are even cases where it shows that there is 1 minute left and that 1 minute ends up being 30." (04)

- "For example, last time, some girl's washing machine showed one minute remaining, and she knew I was waiting for it. So I asked her, “Is that yours?” and she said yes. So I'm sitting there waiting, and that one minute turned into like 15–20 minutes. Then she took her clothes out, and I was like, “Okay, I'll put mine in now.” But then she pulled out another huge bag and said, “Sorry, I still have more clothes to wash.” So basically, I sat there for 15 minutes for nothing, like an idiot." (05; English translation)
- "Finding an available washing machine usually takes the most time, probably around an hour. I mean, there are only five washing machines in total, and just think about how many students there are." (05; English translation)
- "There are many times when I have to go up and down to look for free washing machines or wait for them to end and that of course takes a lot of time." (06)
- "Somehow knowing whether machines are available or not would help so much as that is major problem why my time is wasted and I would save much time knowing when to go down. " (06)

## Pattern 3

**Pattern:** When students leave their laundry uncollected, others must wait or move their clothes, creating delays, discomfort, and concerns about damaging personal belongings.

**Interviews behind it:** 01, 02, 03, 04, 05, 06

**Quotes:**

- "I've encountered situations where laundry was left unattended. In those cases, I used to leave a sticker indicating which dryer I had moved the clothes to. And whenever I've taken someone else's clothes out of a dryer, I've always folded them." (01)
- "I think this kind of action is reasonable. When people share a washing machine and there's a specific amount of time for using it, the owner has a responsibility to collect their laundry on time once the cycle finishes. Since it's a shared facility, I also have the right to remove the laundry, as long as I handle it properly." (01)
- "For me, the problem is waiting for other people. You have to keep checking when they'll take their laundry out so you can put yours in, or wait for the cycle to finish and for the owner to come downstairs." (01)
- "I usually wait for 10 minutes, and if the owner doesn't come to collect their laundry, I move it myself." (01)
- "I usually wait 20–30 minutes for people to collect their clothes, and if they do not come, I take them out myself as i have no other choice because of others lack of responsibility." (02)
- "I do not place the clothes which i took out from the washing machine in the dryer, as some people may not want it and because dryers tend to make clothes smaller sometimes, which actually happened to my friend. someone took his clothes from the washing machine and threw them into the dryer which caused his 300 GEL T-shirt to shrink and become unwearable for him." (02)
- "There are so many of us. I won't even start talking about people's behavior, but six washing machines for this many people, three of them broken — what am I supposed to do? Everyone is waiting in line. If you're five minutes late, they'll take your laundry out and then you have to wash it again. That's not acceptable. I'm very dissatisfied." (03)
- "I always write in the group chat. Taking someone else's laundry out isn't pleasant or hygienic. I don't think it's a pleasant experience for either the person whose clothes are being removed or the person removing them." (03)
- "I've taken out unattended laundry, but I haven't left it unattended. I've moved it to a dryer and turned it on." (03)
- "I've also considered that someone might not want their clothes dried in a dryer, but I can't just leave their clothes wet." (03)
- "I've even waited half an hour, sitting right there and waiting." (03)
- "and other people's laundry gets taken out because there aren't enough washing machines for you to, for example, use a different one." (04)
- "everyone, I think, has times when something unexpected happens or when you can't get back from the lecture in time or, I don't know, you just go down to check the times and minutes get added for no reason, so you can't stay down there for long." (04)
- "It is only humane that when someone goes down to wash their clothes, they should be bringing them up clean, so, if you must, you should take out their clothes and lay them on the chair cleanly, now when you put it on top of the washing machine, or when you take it and put it somewhere else, or when you mix it with someone else's laundry, that is a very big display of irresponsibility." (04)
- "Something along those lines happened to me before and it vas very unpleasant." (04)
- "Well nothing major, I was just 5 minutes late, I was doing the dishes and when I finished I went down to the laundry room to move my clothes to the dryer and well my clothes were nowhere to be seen. I then spotted them on top of a different washing machine mixed up with clothes of other people that was maybe lost or something, I only got a portion of my clothes back that day, many of them I still don't know where they are." (04)

- "Basically, there’s almost always laundry left in the machines. You just don’t really pay attention to it when there are free machines available. But when all the machines are occupied, that’s when you start noticing the laundry people have left behind. So yeah, there’s pretty much always at least one machine with clothes left in it, sometimes two or three on average." (05; English translation)
- "Usually, I just go back upstairs and check again the next time I come down to see if the laundry is gone. But if someone really doesn't bother picking up their clothes and clearly doesn't care about their laundry, then yeah, I've taken their clothes out of the machine a few times." (05; English translation)
- "I usually go back upstairs and give them about an hour to pick up their laundry. And yeah, if I do take someone’s clothes out, I at least put them on a chair. But the other day, some girl took someone else’s laundry out right in front of me and literally dragged it across the floor." (05; English translation)
- "Most of the time, I go downstairs before the washing cycle finishes. I usually set a timer for a little less time than the cycle takes, and then I go down.." (05; English translation)
- "I think it’s perfectly reasonable to take someone’s laundry out after waiting about 5–10 minutes. If anything, I’m the unreasonable one for waiting an entire hour." (05; English translation)
- "I don’t have a problem with someone taking my laundry out of the machine. It depends on how they do it. I mean, I’m fine with it as long as the person does it respectfully and handles my clothes properly." (05; English translation)
- "Roughly, I wait for 10-15 minutes until someone comes for their left behind clothes, and if they do not I have to put them out myself to not waste any more time because of someone else." (06)
- "Actually there was one time when my own clothes were removed by someone else, because I had some private issues and could not get them on time and I went in the laundry room 15-20 minutes late and found half of my clothes dropped on the floor, which of course needed another wash afterwards. " (06)
- "Of course I am not happy about touching someone's clothes and moving them myself, but what can we do, some people just do not leave u any other choice." (06)
- "There was 2 or 3 times when I could not make it in time and one time I told u what happened but on other 2, they had just left my clothes on the chair which is completely okay, as I do not think that someone should wait for me to come down and get my clothes for a long time, no matter the reason, so yea as long as it is done properly I do not have any problems with it." (06)

## Pattern 4

**Pattern:** Limited numbers of usable machines, faults, and poor maintenance increase waiting and sometimes force students to handle technical problems themselves.

**Interviews behind it:** 01, 03, 04, 05

**Quotes:**

- "Some washing machines have problems, so even if they're empty, you can't use them. That's why I prefer bringing my laundry when there's nobody else around." (01)
- "My friends have problems when there are no other available machines and there are washing machines that should be working but aren't. That's the problem — when you should be able to wash your clothes, but the machine doesn't work." (01)
- "In my opinion, more washing machines are needed. The demand is much higher, and the number of washing machines downstairs is not enough for the number of students living in the building." (01)
- "I've had a situation where the washing cycle finished, but there was still water inside and the clothes hadn't been spun properly, so I had to wring everything out by hand. The door wouldn't open because the water hadn't drained, so I had to pull out the hose, drain the water, and barely managed to get my clothes back. Is that normal?" (03)
- "The solution to this problem is to replace the equipment, or when something is broken, have the maintenance staff come and fix it." (03)
- "You shouldn't have to know which machine is broken. None of them should be broken, especially when there are only six." (03)
- "Another problem is that the instructions say you shouldn't use washing powder and should use liquid detergent instead, but some people don't follow that rule, and that's why the machines break down." (03)
- "There are lots of times when the washing machines are broken." (04)
- "For this many students and a dorm building of this size, in my opinion, there are too few washing machines." (04)

- "I’d add more dryers and washing machines, but honestly, I’d throw all the ones we have now straight in the trash. Not a single one of them is any good.." (05; English translation)
- "Another thing that I think is absolutely necessary is that the washing machines and dryers should be cleaned or maintained regularly, either at the end of each day or at certain intervals. Because some of them are in such a disgusting condition that I don't even feel like washing my clothes in them. Sometimes I think my clothes would be better off staying dirty than being washed in those machines. I've gone down there and seen them in that state so many times. I don't know, the situation is just terrible." (05; English translation)
- "Finding an available washing machine usually takes the most time, probably around an hour. I mean, there are only five washing machines in total, and just think about how many students there are." (05; English translation)
- "I mean, that’s also just a human factor. You can’t always expect people to be on time. It’s happened to me too, when I couldn’t go down and pick up my laundry on time. So the real problem isn’t that someone forgets to put their laundry in or take it out or something like that. The main issue is that there are simply too few washing machines." (05; English translation)

## Pattern 5

**Pattern:** Unreliable dryers and concerns about clothing damage lead students to restart cycles, switch machines, or dry clothes elsewhere.

**Interviews behind it:** 02, 03, 04, 05

**Quotes:**

- "Sometimes the dryers turn off by themselves or do not dry the clothes properly, so I have to turn them on again, which is frustrating as it takes a lot of time and requires you to be in the laundry room to check if it is working properly. " (02)
- "Waiting takes up a lot of time. Otherwise, the washing machine finishes its job on time. The dryer sometimes has problems and doesn't dry the clothes properly. Technical problems and waiting are what make the process take longer." (03)
- "Sometimes you've washed your clothes, but there's no dryer available." (03)
- "There was a time when I was waiting for a dryer. Someone came downstairs, opened it, saw that their clothes weren't dry, put them back in, and started it again. In that situation, I took my clothes upstairs and dried them at home." (03)
- "The problem with the dryer is that it sometimes stops by itself after you turn it on. You think it's working, but it's actually off. When you set it for an hour, it can stop after just 10 minutes. You think your clothes have been drying for an hour, but when you go downstairs and open it, they're still damp or wet. You've gone downstairs for nothing." (03)
- "There was a time when I put my clothes in a dryer and left them there. When I came back downstairs, it was still spinning, but when I opened it and checked, the clothes were just as wet as before. So I moved them to another dryer and started the cycle again." (03)
- "Some people even dry their clothes in their own rooms, because of some problems with the dryers you know, some clothes get shrunk in the dryers and so on." (04)

- "I’d add more dryers and washing machines, but honestly, I’d throw all the ones we have now straight in the trash. Not a single one of them is any good.." (05; English translation)

## Pattern 6

**Pattern:** Students respond differently to repeated laundry problems: some continue using the facilities despite difficulties, while others stop using them altogether.

**Interviews behind it:** 01, 03

**Quotes:**

- "Although some washing machines have problems, I'm still satisfied that I have the opportunity to wash my clothes, because the broken machines don't really inconvenience me that much." (01)
- "At first, I used that space quite often, but because of certain problems, I no longer go down to that floor." (03)
- "I haven't been to that floor at all this year. I usually take my clothes to Tbilisi to wash them." (03)
- "I am discouraged about everything" (03)
- "I've wasted so much time and it's been really stressful. Imagine waiting when you've already planned your time — you know when you're supposed to study or when you have a seminar — and then you end up having to wring out all your clothes by hand. These things are a problem nowadays. We've even got flying cars, and I'm still supposed to wring my clothes out by hand?!" (03)

## Pattern 7

**Pattern:** Students initially struggle with washing-machine controls but learn how to use the equipment through friends or experience.

**Interviews behind it:** 01, 02

**Quotes:**

- "When I first moved here, I didn't know what all the washing machine modes were for, but my friends later taught me how to use them." (01)
- "At first, there was a period when I did not know how to use washing machines or dryers but then I learned, and now there is almost nothing I do not know about usage of them." (02)

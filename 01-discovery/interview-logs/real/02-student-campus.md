Interview 02 · KIU Student, Dorm F · Irakli
(Interview was done in English)

Week: 2 · Asker: G.P. · Logger: N.J. and A.G. · Consent: yes; notes and recordings · Real or practice: Real

Context: Third-year Computer Science student at KIU University, living in Building F dormitory. Campus life helps him develop social connections, friendships, and personal responsibility, but the dormitory laundry facilities remain a major source of frustration.
Quotes: 
- "I would say I wash my clothes once a week, always at the end of the week."
- "At first, there was a period when I did not know how to use washing machines or dryers but then I learned, and now there is almost nothing I do not know about usage of them."
- "I mostly go in the mornings because that is when the machines are free and therefore washing them is easier for me, but I do not specifically wake up early in the morning for that reason."
- "I have to go up and down several times during some intervals to check if some machines are free"
- "I usually wait 20–30 minutes for people to collect their clothes, and if they do not come, I take them out myself as i have no other choice because of others lack of responsibility."
- "I do not place the clothes which i took out from the washing machine in the dryer, as some people may not want it and because dryers tend to make clothes smaller sometimes, which actually happened to my friend. someone took his clothes from the washing machine and threw them into the dryer which caused his 300 GEL T-shirt to shrink and become unwearable for him."
- "Sometimes the dryers turn off by themselves or do not dry the clothes properly, so I have to turn them on again, which is frustrating as it takes a lot of time and requires you to be in the laundry room to check if it is working properly. "
- "I do not have any ways to know whether washing machines are free rather than going to the laundry room and check it myself and having something that shows whether the washing machines or dryers are free, would make laundry days much easier and save a lot of time."

Workaround: Visits the laundry room in the mornings, checks machine availability manually, and sets a timer to collect his clothes as soon as possible. If someone leaves their washed clothes in a machine, he reports usually waiting 20–30 minutes before moving them to a chair. He does not transfer other people's clothes into dryers because they might shrink or be damaged.  

Cost: Lost time from repeatedly going up and down to check machine availability, waiting for other students, and dealing with unreliable dryers. These interruptions affect his work and productivity.

Frequency: Does laundry approximately once a week. He estimates that, over one year, there have been around 20–30 occasions when students left their clothes in machines for too long. He typically waits 20–30 minutes for other students to collect their clothes.
Specific incident: One student left clothes in a washing machine for an entire day. In another case, someone's clothes were transferred into a dryer and shrank; one of his friends had to throw away a shirt worth 300 GEL because it no longer fit.


Prediction check (post-interview clarification): This interview provides evidence that unattended completed laundry causes waiting and inconvenience, but it does not establish how long owners typically take to collect their laundry after a cycle ends. The original 15-minute falsification condition remains unverified.

Surprise ★: The problem is not only the availability of machines. Students also leave clothes unattended, some machines are broken, and dryers can be unreliable. Irakli avoids putting other people's clothes in dryers because he does not want to damage them. He also mentioned that he never touches girls' clothes and waits for them to collect them.

Behavioral evidence: During his first year at KIU, he had to wake up early specifically to do laundry because of limited machine availability. He believes the situation has improved mainly because fewer students are currently living in the dorms, rather than because the laundry service itself has improved.

Commitment: Introduced 2 of his friends who also tend to use the laundry room very often and may have useful insights. 

Next: Investigate how other dormitory residents handle occupied machines, waiting times, broken dryers, and checking availability. Ask about their most recent experience rather than whether they would use a proposed solution.

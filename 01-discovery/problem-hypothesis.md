## Step 2: The hypothesis we test first

> **We believe** Luka, 20, a KIU student living on campus who regularly uses the shared dormitory washing machines,
> **struggle with** finding washing machines occupied by completed laundry that has not been picked up
> **when** they bring their laundry to the shared laundry area and find that completed laundry is still inside the washing machines,
> **which costs them** time waiting for a washing machine to become available or returning later.
> **Today they** wait for the owner to collect their laundry, return later, or sometimes remove the completed laundry from the machine.
> **We are wrong if** completed laundry is usually picked up within 15 minutes after the washing cycle ends

## After interviews (fill in Week 3)

**Verdict:** weakened · **Evidence:** Interviews 01, 02, 03, 04, 05, 06. Students described waiting for uncollected laundry, sometimes moving it themselves, and cases where belongings were damaged, lost, or placed on the floor. Interview 05 reports sometimes waiting about an hour before removing clothes, while Interview 06 generally waits 10–15 minutes. These observations do not establish how long completed laundry is **usually** left inside machines, because a student's choice to wait does not measure the owner's collection time. Interview 05 also describes the low number of usable machines as the larger problem. Our prediction therefore remains weakened, rather than confirmed or disproved. See `DECISIONS.md`.

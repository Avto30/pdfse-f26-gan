## Step 2: The hypothesis we test first

> **We believe** Luka, 20, a KIU student living on campus who regularly uses the shared dormitory washing machines,
> **struggles with** finding washing machines occupied by completed laundry that has not been picked up
> **when** he brings his laundry to the shared laundry area and finds that completed laundry is still inside the washing machines,
> **which costs him** time waiting for a washing machine to become available or returning later.
> **Today he** waits for the owner to collect their laundry, returns later, or sometimes removes the completed laundry from the machine.
> **We are wrong if** completed laundry is usually picked up within 15 minutes after the washing cycle ends.

## Step 3: The beliefs we did not pick

Each one becomes at least one question in our interview script, so we still hear about it.

- **Not enough washing machines for the number of students (Avtandili)** → Have you ever encountered a situation where all washing machines/dryers were occupied? If so, how many times has this happened? When was the last time? How long did you wait? (Interview Script v2, Question 13.1)
- **Not knowing the current availability of washing machines wastes students' time and effort (Nita)** → Do you have any way of finding out whether a washing machine/dryer is available? How did you check availability the last time you needed one? (Interview Script v2, Question 13.2)

## After interviews — Week 3 initial verdict and Week 4 recheck

**Verdict:** weakened.

**Evidence:** Interviews 01, 02, 03, 04, 05, 06.

Students described waiting for uncollected laundry, sometimes moving it themselves, and incidents where belongings were damaged, lost, or left on the floor.

Interview 05 reports sometimes waiting about an hour before removing clothes, while Interview 06 generally waits 10–15 minutes. However, these observations do not establish how long completed laundry is **usually** left inside machines, because a student's choice to wait does not measure the owner's collection time.

Interview 05 also describes the insufficient number of usable machines as the larger problem. This suggests that unattended laundry is one part of a wider machine-availability problem.

Our prediction therefore remains **weakened**, rather than confirmed or disproved.
